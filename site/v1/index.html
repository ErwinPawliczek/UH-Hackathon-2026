    The problem

    Plants are optimized alone. They fail together.

    Every site runs its own planning model. Nobody models the links between them, and the links are where the Gulf Coast breaks: a closed channel, a dry river, a tripped cracker forty miles away.

    Coupled

    One fractionation hub feeds dozens of crackers.

    Ethane and propane from Mont Belvieu reach plants from Freeport to Lake Charles. A feed upset there shows up everywhere downstream.

    Fragile

    Waterways close with the weather.

    Hurricanes shut the Houston Ship Channel and the Intracoastal Waterway. Drought drops the Mississippi until barges load light.

    Blind

    Planners find out after the fact.

    Spreadsheets cannot answer what happens to Baton Rouge when Baytown trips. PetroChain answers it in seconds.

    The model

    From unit operation to supply chain.

    Each plant is a real process model with mass balances and capacities. Each route carries real throughput limits and transit times. The simulator steps them forward together.

    Plant agents · in development

    Every plant gets a planner.

    Each site is run by an agent that sees what that site would see: its own inventory, its contracts, the routes it can reach and the prices on the board. They negotiate through the network, not around it.

    Observe

    Local state only. Tank levels, feed nominations, route status and spot prices.

    Decide

    Within physics. Run rate, feed slate and routing, bounded by turndown and capacity.

    Act

    Through the sim. Orders move on real routes with real transit times.

    Expand

    Long horizon. Propose debottlenecks or new units where the network is short.
