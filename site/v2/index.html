    The problem

    Plants are optimized alone. They fail together.

    When something breaks on this coast, each plant has hours to decide whether to cut rates, shut down or buy elsewhere. It decides without knowing how long it has before the failure reaches it.

    Concentrated

    One coast holds almost all US ethylene capacity, so a regional storm is a national supply event. [1]

    02

    Coupled

    Plants here are each other's suppliers and customers. A month after the 2021 freeze, the coast had still not fully recovered. [3]

    Blind

    Planning models cover one site in steady operation. None shows a failure moving from one company to the next. [5]

    Late

    A plant that cannot see what is coming cannot choose between cutting early and running on. Its customers find out by force majeure letter. [4]

    What we do

    A forecast for the supply chain.

    PetroGrid simulates the whole coast as one network, hour by hour. For any disruption, every plant gets three answers.

    01 · Time to impact

    How long it has

    The days until a plant must cut rates or shut down, counted from the moment something breaks anywhere in the network.

    02 · Cost

    What it costs

    Lost production for each plant and for the network, measured against the same coast with nothing broken.

    03 · Response

    Its best move

    The same event run under different choices: run on, cut early or buy elsewhere. The outcomes sit side by side.

    The model

    From unit operation to supply chain.

    Each plant is a process model with mass balances and capacities. Each route carries throughput limits and transit times. The simulator steps them forward together.

    01 · Units

    Plants as process models

    Crackers, fractionators, refineries and polymer lines with yields, nameplate capacity and turndown limits.

    ktabbl/dyield slates
    02 · Routes

    Real geography

    Pipelines, the Intracoastal Waterway, the Mississippi, highways and export lanes, each with capacity and lead time.

    pipelinebargeshiptruck
    03 · Events

    Disruptions propagate

    Close a channel or trip a unit. Inventories draw down, feeds reroute and run rates fall through the network hour by hour.

    inventoryrerouting
    04 · Agents

    Plants that decide

    An AI agent per plant sets run rates, buys feed, picks routes and proposes expansions against the same constraints.

    Who it is for

    A private forecast, built on a public model.

    The model starts from public data, so it is useful before anyone signs up. A company that registers its plants gets a sharper forecast that only it can see.

    01 · Public model

    The whole coast

    Plant capacities, pipelines, waterways and ports, assembled from public sources.

    02 · Private overlay

    Your plants, your numbers

    Register a site and enter its real capacities and tank levels. Your forecast sharpens, and nobody else sees your data or your answer.

    03 · Network effect

    Better for everyone

    Each registered plant improves the estimates beneath every forecast, without revealing whose data did it.

    Three kinds of customer

    Anyone exposed to the network

    Producers come first: one avoided day of cracker downtime is worth more than a site licence. Buyers, insurers and public agencies follow.

    Producers' supply plannersA private forecast and response options for each plant
    Resin and chemical buyersEarly warning on which suppliers are exposed
    Insurers, ports and agenciesNetwork-wide scenarios and restart sequencing
    Plant agents · in development

    Every plant gets a planner.

    Each site is run by an agent that sees what that site would see: its own inventory, its contracts, the routes it can reach and the prices on the board. They negotiate through the network, not around it.

    Observe
    Local state only. Tank levels, feed nominations, route status and spot prices.
    Decide
    Within physics. Run rate, feed slate and routing, bounded by turndown and capacity.
    Act
    Through the sim. Orders move on real routes with real transit times.
    Expand
    Long horizon. Propose debottlenecks or new units where the network is short.
